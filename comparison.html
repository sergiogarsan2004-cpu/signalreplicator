The column missing from every other comparison

Every comparison in this sector compares the same three things: price, speed and supported platforms. None of them compares who ends up holding your broker password, which is the decision with the biggest consequences of all.

To trade on your MetaTrader account you need the master password, not the read-only one. Whoever holds it can open and close trades on your account. They cannot take your money out — withdrawals are requested through the broker's portal, not the terminal — but they can trade as much as they like.

An independent industry comparison puts it like this:

"All copiers require sharing broker login credentials with third-party services. Reputable providers cannot withdraw funds, but they retain access to account trading."

That sentence is true of most of them. It is not true of all of them, and the difference lies in how each product is built.

The three ways of connecting to MetaTrader

MetaTrader 4 and 5 have no official API. MetaQuotes offers no standard way for an outside service to trade on your account. That leaves only three routes, and each one has its toll:

1. Terminals in the provider's cloud

The service runs MetaTrader on its own servers and logs in with your credentials: login, master password and server. Your computer can be switched off. It is the cheapest option, because one more customer costs the provider almost nothing. In exchange, your credentials live on the infrastructure of a company you do not know.

2. Through MetaApi

MetaApi is a company that specialises in giving programmatic access to MetaTrader accounts. The copier does not talk to your broker: it talks to MetaApi. If the MetaApi account is yours, you enter your credentials there and the copier never sees them. It costs more, because there is a third party charging per connected account.

3. A program on your own computer

The copier installs on your machine and trades against your local MetaTrader. Nothing leaves your computer. In exchange, the machine has to stay switched on, or you pay for a VPS.

What we learned reading everyone else's reviews

Trade2Sync is the biggest in the sector: it claims more than 90,000 users and has 475 Trustpilot reviews averaging 4.5. But that average hides something: 85% five-star and 10% one-star. People love it or hate it, with almost nothing in between.

The one-star complaints repeat across three themes, and they are worth reading before you choose any copier, ours included:

  • Slowness and missed signals. "Very slow execution and misses many signals."
  • Nobody knows whose fault it was. Support always says the problem is the signal provider's, not the copier's, and doesn't help resolve anything.
  • Hard to cancel. Users report trying to cancel, getting no answer on how, and still being charged.

And almost every good review talks about the same thing: a person who helped them set it up. This is not a market the cheapest option wins. It is won by whoever gets the customer set up and able to see it working.

On speed, and why almost nobody gives numbers

Every copier promises to be fast. Almost none publishes a measurement.

We ran a load test: 500 messages in 10 minutes against a real group. The time between a signal appearing in Telegram and the order sitting at the broker was 195 milliseconds median, with 579 ms in the worst 5% of cases.

We are not saying we are the fastest on the market: we have not measured the others under the same conditions, and anyone who claims otherwise without doing so is selling you smoke. We are saying what we measured, how, and when.
